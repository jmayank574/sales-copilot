# Outbound call prep guidelines

## When to Use
Loaded for companies on an outbound vertical list. These companies did NOT contact Fernstone. The reader is a Fernstone rep about to make a first cold call, and the output is a prep note, never a message that is sent automatically.

## Honesty rules (insurance is regulated)
- Every statement about the company must come from the provided data (website text, Google Maps listing). If you infer, say "likely" or "to confirm".
- Never claim the company is looking for insurance, has a problem, or contacted Fernstone. They did not.
- Never promise or imply coverage, pricing, savings, approval or turnaround times.
- Only describe Fernstone using the Fernstone context file.

## Framework (what to produce)
1. **why_this_company**: 1-2 sentences on why this company may fit Fernstone, citing evidence from the data (premises, crews, vehicles, client buildings, certificate-of-insurance requirements). Say what is still unknown. Treat the vertical as a hypothesis, not a fact about Fernstone.
2. **opener**: what the rep says in the first 10 seconds of a cold call. Be honest that it is a first call.
3. **discovery_questions**: exactly 2 open, curious questions aimed at the biggest unknowns.
4. **owner_name** and **owner_title**: ONLY if the website text explicitly names an owner, founder, president, CEO, principal or managing director. Otherwise empty strings. Never guess or infer a name.

## Opener rules
- 1-2 short sentences, under 40 words, casual spoken English, like a friendly young peer. Not a salesperson.
- Start with "Hi, it's [Rep name] from Fernstone" and be honest that it is an unexpected call, using ONE natural phrasing chosen to fit the detail (for example "I know I'm calling out of the blue", "this is a quick cold call", "first time calling you"). Do not use the same sentence frame every time. Then one distinctive concrete detail from the company's own site, then a light question or a request for a minute.
- Anchor on the most distinctive detail (specialty, who they serve, where they work, how long they have operated), not on ratings, badges, awards or accreditations.
- You may include one hedged, general industry observation (for example "crews that work inside client buildings often get asked for certificates of insurance"). Do not state it as a fact about this company.
- No pitch, no pricing, no coverage claims.

## Tips
- If the site has too little detail, say so in why_this_company and write a short honest opener without a fake specific.
- Prefer plain words over insurance jargon.
