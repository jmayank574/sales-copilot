# Fernstone context

## When to Use
Loaded into every scoring and brief prompt. It is the only place Claude may take facts about Fernstone from.

## Source tags
- **[site]** = stated on fernstone.com (page or menu noted).
- **[job post]** = stated in Fernstone's public Growth & Operations Analyst job posting.
- **[assumption]** = my draft, to be confirmed by the founders. Do not treat assumptions as facts.

## What Fernstone is
- [site, home] An insurance brokerage for businesses. "Insurance for businesses that move fast."
- [site, home] Platform for COIs (certificates of insurance), claims, policy management, renewal/expiration alerts, billing.
- [site, working-with-fernstone] Dedicated team that learns the business, guides underwriting, finds carriers for the right balance of price, coverage and terms. Earns nothing unless a policy is placed.
- [job post] Combines AI with expert advisors to place insurance faster and at better terms. Y Combinator-backed. New York City office.

## Verticals Fernstone publishes
- [site, home "Verticals" menu, "Get a quote for"] Security, Garage, Daycares, Staffing, Bars and restaurants, Fire and lifesafety, Pest control, Med spas, Fleet repair, Nightlife, Hotels.
- Individual vertical pages were not found. The list comes from the menu only.

## Traction and sales motion
- [job post] 500+ customers since the start of the year. Average sales cycle of about 15 days. The company is scaling to reach thousands more.
- Implication (inference from the 15-day cycle): speed to first contact matters.

## Coverage lines named
- [site, loss-run-faq] workers' compensation, general liability, commercial auto, property, professional liability, umbrella or excess liability.

## What underwriters need from a business
- [site, loss-run-faq] Operations, payroll, revenue, vehicles, locations, coverage needs, and claims history (loss runs).

## Carrier partners and stated appetite
- [site, home] Tokio Marine (global capacity), AmTrust (comp specialist), Markel (specialty), Liberty Mutual (broad appetite), The Hartford (middle market), CNA (professional lines), Travelers (national), BHSI (specialty), Kinsale (specialty).

## Stated good fit
- [site, working-with-fernstone] Wants to save money without sacrificing coverage; knows the business inside and out; takes pride in a strong, well-managed organization; values a straightforward broker relationship; shares information and responds to document requests quickly.

## Stated poor fit
- [site, working-with-fernstone] Wants the fastest quote regardless of price, coverage or accuracy; will not share meaningful business details; unwilling to give underwriters needed information.

## NOT stated publicly
- Target company size.
- Supported states or geography.
- Customer proof points or case studies. (The "98% claims processed" figure is in a dashboard mockup on the homepage and must not be used as a claim.)
- Which verticals are strongest or newest.

## Draft assumptions (confirm with founders)
- [assumption] The listed verticals share a pattern: small and mid-size local businesses with premises, crews or vehicles, where liability, workers' comp and similar exposures are material.
- [assumption] A business is "adjacent" if it fits that pattern but is not in the published list (for example electrical or HVAC contractors, trucking companies).
- [assumption] Not a fit: individuals, tiny solo operators with little exposure, and large enterprises with in-house risk teams.
- [assumption] Owner, founder, CEO, president or CFO is the ideal contact.
- [assumption] Geography is not scored until the founders list supported states.
